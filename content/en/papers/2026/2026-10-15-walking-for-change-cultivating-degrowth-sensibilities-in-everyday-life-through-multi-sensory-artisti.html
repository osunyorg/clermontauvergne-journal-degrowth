There is an increasing acknowledgement that the type and scale of change implied by a societal transition beyond growth cannot be triggered only by scientific facts or macroeconomic perspectives. Change is also needed in collective imaginaries, cultural values, and practices. This requires situating the transformative process in everyday life and the lived realities of communities to collectively imagine a life beyond consumerism as possible and desirable. Artistic practices can play an important role in subverting hegemonic worldviews through different aesthetic propositions. In this article, I focus on their potential to shape alternative imaginaries and foster degrowth-aligned values by enhancing awareness of alternative paradigms through multi-sensory engagements in everyday public spaces that expose frictions. I do this by drawing on a collaboration with an artist collective involving a co-created walking-based method (‘walkshop’) carried out in a relatively new neighbourhood of Utrecht, The Netherlands. Reflecting on this experience, I propose situated civic-artistic pedagogies to cultivate degrowth sensibilities in everyday life.
