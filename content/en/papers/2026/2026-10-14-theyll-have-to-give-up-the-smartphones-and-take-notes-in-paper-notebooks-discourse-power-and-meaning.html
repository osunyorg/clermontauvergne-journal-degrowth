By analysing 38 articles published shortly after the event, the study identifies a polarized media landscape structured around three dominant narrative framings: traditionalist‑populist, paternalist‑denialist, and ecological‑transformative. These narratives portray degrowth as a threat to national prosperity and identity, dismissed as impractical or naïve, or framed as a legitimate response to ecological and social pressures. Situating these representations within Croatia’s semi-peripheral, post‑socialist context, the analysis shows how media framings intersect with long‑standing developmental aspirations, public attitudes toward environmental and social justice, and local political dynamics.
